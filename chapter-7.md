# Chapter 7: Stronghold Run
## *"Fate points down."*

---

The briefing table was covered in materials — blaze rods still warm from the Nether, a pouch of ender pearls pulsing with captured void, and a single shimmering Eye of Ender glowing at the center.

Stone picked up a pearl. Colder than it should be. Like holding a piece of the night sky.

"What are these for?"

Red stood at the head of the table. There was a weight in his eyes that Stone hadn't seen before.

"The big job," Red said. "The one that sets us up for good."

The crew leaned in. Brick cracked his knuckles. Gear adjusted his goggles. Pick was already taking notes.

"There's a structure buried underground," Red continued. "Ancient. Older than anything on the surface. The strongholds were built by something that came before. And at the heart of every stronghold — a portal."

"A portal to where?" Gear asked.

"The End."

Silence. The name echoed off the stone walls. A dimension of void and shadow. A dragon that ruled the sky. A place nobody on this server had ever raided successfully.

"You're talking about the dragon dimension," Pick said flatly.

"Nobody's ever had this crew," Red said.

Gear shifted. "First we find the stronghold. That's the hard part."

Red tossed the Eye to Pick. "You're up. Tell us how we triangulate this thing."

---

Pick spread a map across the table, coordinates marked in precise handwriting. "Eyes of Ender are simple. You throw one, it floats toward the stronghold for about twelve blocks, then either drops or breaks. You follow the direction, throw again, adjust course."

"How many throws?" Stone asked.

"Depends on distance. Some pop on impact — maybe one in five. The survivors, you pick up and reuse." Pick pulled out a notebook of equations. "I've calculated the optimal pattern. We find the angle from two different throw locations, plot the intersection on the map, and get the stronghold coordinates within twenty blocks."

"Twenty blocks of error's a lot," Brick said.

"Not when the structure can span a hundred blocks underground."

Gear counted materials. "We've got twelve blaze rods from the Nether run. Each makes two blaze powder. Each powder plus one pearl makes an Eye. Pearls are the bottleneck — we've got six."

"Six Eyes," Red said. "The portal needs twelve. We'd better find that stronghold fast."

---

## The Hunt Begins

They set out at dawn. Plains gave way to dark oak forests, rivers cutting through the landscape like silver veins. Pick threw the first Eye at the edge of a sunflower field.

It launched with a soft *whoosh*, trailing sparkling particles, oriented itself, and drifted southeast.

"Got a heading," Pick said, plucking it off the grass. "Still intact. At least five hundred blocks."

They walked. The sun climbed overhead. Gear marked their coordinates on a scrap of paper while Brick kept watch for mobs that might wander into their path. The Eye led them through two biomes before Pick called for a second throw.

Two hundred blocks later, he tossed again. The Eye soared higher this time, its particle trail longer and brighter before it dropped to the grass and shattered on impact.

"Lost one. Seven left."

The crew exchanged glances. Seven Eyes to find a structure that could be eight hundred blocks out, and twelve needed to open the portal once they reached it.

"Keep going," Red said. "Find the stronghold first."

The third throw led them into a roofed forest where the canopy swallowed the sky. The Eye wove between trunks like a ghost, light filtering through the leaves in scattered beams. At the edge of a clearing, Pick threw the fourth — and a stray skeleton arrow caught it mid-flight. The Eye burst in a shower of green sparks, particles raining down like tears.

Pick stared at the drifting remnants. "That's not possible."

Brick doubled over laughing. "The skeleton shot your Eye!"

"A skeleton cannot target a thrown item!" Pick's face was reddening. "That violates entity targeting rules!"

"Tell that skeleton."

The skeleton stood at the edge of the clearing, bow still raised, skull tilted as if it knew exactly what it had done. Stone put an arrow through its ribcage. The bones clattered to the forest floor.

Gear counted quietly. "Five Eyes left."

The laughter died.

---

At the eight hundred block mark, Pick threw the fifth Eye. It rose thirty blocks into the air, hung suspended, and then fell straight down, burying itself in the dirt.

"We're here," Pick said. "Stronghold's directly below us."

Stone looked at the grass beneath his boots. Somewhere in the dark, an ancient structure waited.

"Dig."

---

## The Stronghold

Gear found the first mossy brick at Y=42. The air that rose from the broken ceiling was stale and cold — the breath of centuries.

Red dropped first, sword in one hand, torch in the other. "It's huge. I can't even see the end."

The stronghold was a maze. Corridors branched in every direction — some ending in sudden drops, others opening into vast chambers. Cracked stone bricks. Moss-covered walls. Cobwebs draped the ceilings like burial shrouds. Their torches cast shadows that made every corner look like it held something waiting.

"This place is older than anything I've ever seen," Gear whispered. The enchanted redstone lines on his chestplate reflected the flickering torchlight.

"How do we find the portal?" Brick asked.

"Portal rooms sit at the end of main corridors," Pick said. "Pick a direction, mark every turn. If we hit a dead end, backtrack."

"And the silverfish," Stone added.

"The what?"

The wall next to Brick's head *cracked*. A block of mossy stone split open and a gray segmented creature burst out, skittering up the wall. It bit at Brick's arm before he could react.

Brick crushed it against the wall. More blocks cracked. More silverfish poured out — a dozen, two dozen, their chittering filling the corridor like a living wave.

"RUN!"

They sprinted through three corridors, a spiral staircase, and a flooded intersection before the silverfish gave up, retreating into their stone shells. The crew collapsed against a wall, gasping.

"I hate this place," Brick said.

---

## The Library

They found the library by accident.

Stone had been marking their path — torches on the right wall only — when the corridor opened into a cavernous room, two stories high, filled with bookshelves stretching into darkness. A dry fountain sat in the center, its basin thick with dust.

Gear's eyes went wide. "Enchanted books. If any have decent enchantments—"

"We're not here to loot," Red said, though even his voice was softer than usual.

Gear pointed to the far end of the library. Behind a curtain of cobwebs, a doorway led into a smaller chamber.

They crossed the library slowly. The doorway opened, and there it was.

The End Portal.

Twelve blocks of ornate dark stone arranged in a circle, each topped with a recessed slot. The center was nothing but blackness — an absence so complete that looking at it felt like falling.

"Twelve slots," Pick said quietly. "We've got five Eyes."

Red's jaw tightened. "We need more pearls."

They stood in silence, staring at the empty frame. So close. Seven short.

"Endermen," Stone said finally.

---

## The Ender Pearl Hunt

Night had fallen over the surface. A new moon. Perfect conditions.

They didn't have to wait long. A tall figure stood motionless on the plain, its long limbs silhouetted against the stars, purple eyes glowing.

"Don't look at it," Gear cautioned. "Look at its feet."

Stone drew his sword. The Enderman turned its head. Purple eyes locked on.

It screamed — a sound like tearing reality — and teleported behind him. Claws swung. Stone rolled, came up swinging, caught the Enderman's leg. It shrieked and teleported again.

Brick threw a snowball. It hit the Enderman square in the chest. The creature turned, furious, teleporting toward him — right into the path of Pick's arrow.

The Enderman dissolved, leaving a single pulsing ender pearl on the grass.

"Seven to go."

---

The hunt lasted hours. They fought in shifts — two engaging, two resting, one watching for more spawns. Endermen came in ones and twos, sometimes three at a time, each fight a frantic dance of breaking eye contact and striking at the last second.

Stone took a hit to the ribs that cracked his chestplate and sent him sprawling. Brick dragged him back by the collar while Gear and Pick delivered a volley of arrows to the Enderman's torso. Gear circled around, sword drawn, forcing the creature into a line of fire. Pick's arrow punched through its shoulder, and the Enderman dissolved into particles, another pearl hitting the dirt.

"That one almost had your name on it," Brick said, hauling Stone upright.

"Almost."

By the time the moon reached its zenith, they had seven fresh pearls.

Pick worked by torchlight, crushing blaze rods into powder, mixing each portion with an ender pearl until they shimmered green. "Twelve Eyes of Ender. Ready to go."

---

## The Activation

Gear moved around the frame methodically, placing each Eye into its slot with the care of a craftsman.

*Slot one. Slot two. Slot three.*

The Eyes clicked into place, each emitting a soft hum that grew louder. The blackness in the center began to ripple — not filling with light, but *changing*, like water disturbed by something beneath.

*Slot seven. Slot eight. Slot nine.*

The crew gathered around. Stone felt the hum in his teeth. Brick stood with his axe ready. Pick had an arrow nocked. Gear's hands were steady as he placed the final Eyes.

*Slot eleven. Slot twelve.*

The frame erupted.

Purple light exploded from the center — not outward, but inward, as if the void was being consumed by energy. The blackness swirled and *opened*. A gateway. Dark purple, shimmering, pulsing like a distant nebula.

"Portal's active," Gear said, stepping back. "I actually can't believe this worked."

Stone stared into the rift. Beyond it, he knew, was another world. An island of end stone floating in an endless void. Obsidian pillars. A dragon circling the sky.

"Is everyone ready?" Red asked. He looked at each of them in turn. "Because once we step through, there's no coming back until it's done."

Brick cracked his neck. "Didn't walk eight hundred blocks and get bitten by silverfish to chicken out now."

"I've got enough arrows to fill the void," Pick said.

Gear lit a fresh torch. "I'll keep us out of the dark."

Stone met Red's eyes and nodded once.

The purple light cast deep shadows across Red's face. He turned to the portal, one hand reaching toward its surface.

"Alright," Red said. "Who's coming with me?"
