# Chapter 6: The Enchantment

The morning after the diamond haul, Red stood in the center of the base with a piece of charcoal and a flat slab of stone. The crew sat around him nursing bowls of mushroom stew, still sore from the deepslate grind.

"Five diamonds," Red said, tapping the slab. "That's our ticket out of the iron age. But a diamond pickaxe without enchantment is like a car without fuel—looks good, goes nowhere."

Gear was already sketching on his own slab, a coil of redstone dust serving as a paperweight. "The enchantment table needs two diamonds, four obsidian, and a book. Obsidian we have—that lava pool we found last week gave us six blocks. Book's easy: paper and leather."

"I've been working on the room design." Gear flipped the slab around. "Optimal enchanting goes up to level thirty. That requires fifteen bookshelves arranged in a specific pattern—one block of air between the table and the bookshelves."

"Fifteen bookshelves is a lot of cows," Brick groaned.

"Forty-five leather to be exact," Pick said, not looking up from his own calculations. "Plus two hundred and seventy paper. That's about sixteen sugarcane harvested and replanted three times, assuming a fully grown stalk yields one paper worth of sugar."

Red cracked his knuckles. "Stone and Brick, you're on leather duty. There's a plains biome three blocks east—cows as far as the eye can see. Gear, you're with me. We need sugarcane, and we need string."

"String?" Stone raised an eyebrow.

Red smiled. "The enchanting setup isn't complete without a bow. And bows need string. I've got eyes on an abandoned mineshaft under the taiga. Word is there's a cave spider spawner down there."

Stone set his bowl down. "Cave spiders. You're talking about the tiny green ones that poison you and move like greased lightning."

"Poison won't kill you by itself," Red said. "But it'll drop you to half a heart and leave you helpless. The real danger is the swarms. They spawn fast, they're hard to hit, and once you're poisoned, you panic."

"Lovely," Brick muttered.

---

The plains stretched out under a brilliant blue sky, tall grass swaying in the breeze. Stone and Brick crested a hill and spotted the herd—at least thirty cows dotting the landscape, their brown-and-white hides warm in the morning light.

"Thirty cows," Brick said, hefting his stone sword. "We need forty-five leather. Plus extras for mistakes."

"You're not going to kill them all, are you?"

"Not if we're smart." Stone pulled out a bucket. "First, we breed them. Get the herd bigger, then harvest the extras. Sustainable."

Brick looked confused. "Breed them? With what?"

Stone held up a handful of wheat. "You've been living under a block? Cows love wheat. Two cows with wheat makes a baby cow. Baby cow grows up, gives leather. It's basic economics."

For the next hour, Stone played shepherd—luring cows with wheat, leading them into a temporary pen he'd built from fence posts, and expanding the herd until nearly fifty cattle milled about, mooing in protest.

Brick handled the, uh, *harvesting* end of things. He wasn't gentle, but he was efficient. By noon, they had forty-eight pieces of leather stacked in Stone's inventory, plus enough steak to feed the crew for a week.

"Not bad," Stone said, tossing a raw steak to Brick. "You're almost useful."

"Almost?" Brick caught the steak one-handed. "I'm the muscle. You're the brains. Together we're a whole operation."

---

Meanwhile, Red and Gear had found the river. Sugarcane grew along its banks in tall green stalks, their tips brushing against the surface of the water. Gear harvested methodically, cutting each stalk at the base and leaving the bottom block to regrow.

"Forty-five stalks minimum," Gear counted. "That's a lot of planting."

"We'll set up a farm," Red said. "Sugarcane grows fast—one block every eighteen minutes if it's planted on sand or dirt next to water. A small patch with forty-five stalks in a checkerboard pattern will yield a full harvest in three Minecraft days."

Red pulled out a shovel and began digging a trench next to the river. "Water source block here. Dirt rows along the edge. Four rows of twelve should give us forty-eight plants. By the time we're back from the mineshaft, we'll have enough for all nine bookshelves worth of paper."

They built the farm in under an hour—a neat grid of tilled dirt beside the river, each row fed by a water channel. Gear planted the sugarcane stalks carefully, spacing them out for maximum growth.

"Done," Gear said, standing back to admire his work. "Three days and we're golden."

"Good. Now for the fun part."

---

The entrance to the abandoned mineshaft was a hole in the ground, half-hidden by vines and dirt. Red went first, torch in one hand, iron sword in the other. Gear followed, his shield raised and a bucket of milk sloshing at his belt.

"Milk?" Gear asked.

"Cave spiders poison you," Red said. "Milk clears the poison. We drink it, we don't die."

The mineshaft opened up into a vast network of wooden supports and narrow tunnels. The wood was rotten in places, creaking under its own weight. Cobwebs stretched across the corridors, sticky and thick.

"Remember the rule," Red whispered. "Torches on the right going in."

They moved deeper. The air grew stale. Then, from somewhere ahead, a faint *click-clack* echoed through the tunnels. Then another. Then a dozen.

Red held up a fist—*stop.*

Around the corner, the tunnel opened into a large chamber. And in the center, wrapped in a cage of cobwebs, sat a spawner—its iron bars flickering with a faint purple glow, miniature cave spiders rotating around it like satellites around a dark star.

"That's it," Gear breathed.

"Spawners only activate when a player is within sixteen blocks," Red said. "So right now we're safe. The moment we cross that threshold—"

A spider dropped from the ceiling. It was tiny—smaller than a regular spider, with a greenish-blue tint and eyes that glowed an angry red. It landed on Gear's shoulder and bit down.

Gear screamed. The venom hit instantly—his vision blurred, health bar dropping to a single row of half-hearts, green poison hearts appearing on his screen. He stumbled, dropping his torch.

"Drink the milk!" Red shouted, slicing the spider in two with his sword.

Gear fumbled for the bucket, chugging the milk in desperate gulps. The poison cleared. His health stopped draining. But already, more spiders were coming—pouring out of cracks in the walls, dropping from the ceiling, crawling through the cobwebs.

"We're in range of the spawner!" Red yelled. "We need to break it!"

The narrow corridor became a nightmare. Every swing of Red's sword hit a spider—but two more took its place. They were fast, relentless, their bites landing faster than he could drink milk.

Gear grabbed a torch and held it like a weapon. "Spiders hate light! Push forward!"

They advanced, step by agonizing step, clubbing and slashing through the swarm. Red caught a bite on his arm—the poison surged again. He drank. Another bite to the leg. He drank again. His milk supply was running out.

"Five blocks!" Gear shouted. "Four! Three!"

Red lunged for the spawner, his iron sword raised. A spider latched onto his back, fangs sinking into his shoulder. He gritted his teeth, swung—

*CRUNCH.*

The spawner shattered. Its bars crumbled, the purple glow flickering out. The remaining spiders, freed from their spawning source, scattered into the darkness.

Red collapsed against the wall, breathing hard. "Tell me... you got the string."

Gear held up a bundle of white strands, pulled from the webs. "Twenty-three pieces. More than enough for a bow."

Red laughed, a raw, exhausted sound. "Good. Now help me up. We've got an enchantment table to build."

---

Back at the base, the crew assembled.

Stone and Brick had forty-eight leather. The sugarcane farm had yielded its first full harvest—two hundred and eighty paper, enough for nine bookshelves and change. Gear had his string, and Red had the obsidian and diamonds.

Gear laid out the schematic for the enchanting room. "The table goes in the center. Bookshelves go in a ring around it—but not right next to it. There has to be a one-block gap of air between the table and the bookshelf. And they need to be at the same height or one block higher."

"So a horseshoe," Stone said.

"Exactly." Gear drew a U-shape on the floor. "Fifteen bookshelves total. Three rows of three on the left, three rows of three on the right, and three rows of three across the back. The front stays open so you can walk in and use the table."

"This is for Level 30 enchantments," Pick said, his eyes gleaming. "The most powerful you can get without a secondary setup."

They built the bookshelves first. Red took the diamonds and obsidian and crafted the enchantment table at the crafting table—two diamonds on either side of the top row, a diamond in the center, obsidian filling the bottom row and the book at the top center. The table hummed as it took shape, a purple glow emanating from its surface.

"Beautiful," Red whispered.

They placed it in the center of the room, exactly where Gear's schematic showed. Then came the bookshelves—fifteen of them, arranged in the perfect horseshoe pattern. When the last one clicked into place, the enchantment table pulsed, runes swirling across its surface in an ancient script.

"Level thirty," Gear said. "Every enchantment at its maximum potential."

Stone stepped forward. In his hand, he held the diamond pickaxe—the one they'd earned in the deepslate tunnels, forged from the gems Pick had mined at Y=-58.

"This is what it's for," Stone said. "All of it. The branch mining, the cows, the sugarcane, the spider pit... this."

He placed the pickaxe in the left slot of the enchantment table. The table hummed louder, the runes spinning faster. In the right slot, he placed three lapis lazuli—the fuel for the enchantment.

Three options appeared.

Stone scanned them: Efficiency III, Unbreaking III. And as a bonus enchantment—Fortune I.

"The pickaxe already has Efficiency II from the anvil," Red said. "We can combine them. Efficiency III plus Unbreaking III plus Fortune I. That's a god-tier pick."

"How?" Stone asked.

Gear pointed to the anvil they'd placed in the corner—a massive block of iron with a flat top. "You enchant the pickaxe. Then you repair it and upgrade it with the anvil. The anvil costs experience levels, but the result is a legendary tool."

Stone selected the enchantment. The table spun, a shower of purple particles raining down. When it stopped, the pickaxe glowed with a faint, humming energy—enchanted, alive, *powerful.*

Efficiency III. Unbreaking III.

He picked it up. It felt different—lighter, more responsive. He swung it at the cobblestone wall of the base, and the block shattered in a single hit.

"Whoa," Brick said.

"Efficiency III makes it three times faster than an unenchanted diamond pickaxe," Pick said. "And Unbreaking III means it'll last roughly four times longer before breaking. With Mending—if we ever find it—this tool will never die."

Stone cradled the pickaxe like a newborn. "This changes everything."

Red nodded. "That's the idea."

He walked to the map on the wall—the one with X's and question marks. He uncapped his charcoal and drew a large circle in the unexplored territory to the east.

"What's that?" Gear asked.

Red didn't answer immediately. He studied the map, his eyes narrowing.

"Something bigger."

The crew exchanged glances. The room fell silent, the only sound the hum of the enchantment table and the faint crackle of the glowstone lanterns.

"How much bigger?" Stone asked.

Red turned, a glint in his eye. "Stronghold bigger."

The word hung in the air like a thundercloud. Strongholds. The End Portal. The Dragon.

"Find a stronghold," Red said, "and we find the End Portal. Find the End Portal..."

"We fight the Ender Dragon," Gear finished.

Stone looked down at his new pickaxe, the enchantment runes pulsing with purple light. It was the most powerful tool he'd ever held.

And suddenly, it felt like not enough.

**ENCHANTING TIPS FROM THIS CHAPTER:**

1. **The optimal Level 30 enchanting setup** requires exactly 15 bookshelves arranged with a one-block gap of air between the bookshelves and the enchantment table. The most space-efficient pattern is a horseshoe: 5 bookshelves on the left, 5 on the right, 5 across the back, with the front open.

2. **Bookshelves cost one book and three planks each.** Books cost three paper and one leather. So 15 bookshelves = 15 books = 45 leather and 270 paper (from ~90 sugarcane stalks grown over three harvests).

3. **Spawners activate within 16 blocks** of a player. When approaching a spawner room, prepare with milk buckets (clears poison), light sources, and area-of-effect weapons.

4. **Milk clears status effects.** In cave spider fights, always carry a bucket of milk to instantly clear poison. Drink it, don't save it.

5. **The anvil combines enchantments**, repairs items, and renames them. Combining an Efficiency II pick with an Efficiency III book costs levels but yields a superior tool. Plan your upgrades carefully.

6. **The Enchantment Table + Anvil + Grindstone** forms the complete enchanting system. The grindstone removes enchantments to recover some experience from unwanted tools.
