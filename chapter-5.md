# Chapter 5: Diamond Score

The crew gathered in Red's underground base, the glowstone lanterns casting long shadows across the cobblestone floor. A crafting table sat in the center, surrounded by chests overflowing with iron, coal, and the spoils of their last dozen expeditions.

Red stood at the head of the table, arms crossed. Behind him, a map of the underground stretched across the wall, marked with X's where they'd explored and question marks where they hadn't.

"Alright, listen up," Red said, his voice carrying the weight of a man who'd been thinking about this for days. "We've been scraping by with iron. Piglins laugh at us. The End is a joke without better gear. It's time for the big leagues."

Stone leaned against the wall, arms folded. "You're talking about diamonds."

"Three of them," Red said. "Minimum. We need an enchantment table. Without one, we're stuck in the stone age while the world moves on."

Pick stepped forward, unrolling a schematic onto the crafting table. The torchlight flickered over his detailed drawings—grid lines, measurements, and arrows pointing downward.

"Branch mining," Pick said, tapping the diagram with a calloused finger. "Y-level negative fifty-eight. That's the sweet spot for diamond generation since the 1.18 update. We dig down to that layer, then start a main tunnel. From there, we carve side tunnels—two blocks high, one block wide, every third block."

Gear scratched his head. "Every third block? Why not every other? Why not just strip the whole thing?"

"Efficiency." Pick's eyes lit up. "A 2x1 tunnel reveals four blocks per meter of tunnel—ceiling and floor included. By spacing tunnels three blocks apart, you maximize exposed faces per block mined. Every block you don't dig is a block you don't have to replace or light up. It's the highest diamond-per-minute ratio in the game."

Brick snorted. "Sounds like math class. Just dig down and find 'em."

"Yeah, and then a creeper drops on your head and you lose everything," Stone said dryly. "Pick's method works. I've seen it."

Red picked up a pickaxe—iron, well-worn from hours in the stone. "We go to Y=-58. We dig smart. We watch each other's backs. And we come back with diamonds."

---

The descent took twenty minutes. The crew moved in formation: Pick in front reading the coordinates from his F3 menu, Stone behind him with a shield raised, Gear and Brick bringing up the rear with torches and extra pickaxes.

The mine shaft spiraled downward, switchbacks carved into the stone to minimize fall damage. Every few blocks, Gear placed a torch on the right-hand wall—a trick Pick had taught them so they'd always know which way led out. Torches on the right meant heading in. Torches on the left meant heading home.

"Negative forty," Pick called out. "Forty-two. Forty-five. Almost there."

The temperature seemed to drop. The stone grew darker, veins of deepslate streaking through the walls like veins in marble. Lava pools bubbled in distant caves, casting an orange glow that danced across their faces.

"Fifty-eight," Pick said finally. "This is it."

They had landed in a small pocket—walls of deepslate surrounding them, the floor uneven and cracked. Pick immediately began marking the walls with charcoal, drawing lines where the branch tunnels would go.

"Main tunnel goes east," he said, already swinging his pick. "First branch tunnel starts here. We dig twelve blocks, then come back and start the next one three blocks over."

The rhythm was almost meditative. *Clink. Clink. Clink.* Pick's pickaxe bit into the deepslate, slower than stone but just as satisfying. Behind him, Stone collected the cobbled deepslate blocks, stacking them neatly for later use.

"Deepslate's tougher than regular stone," Pick said between swings. "Takes about twice as long to break. But that's where diamonds spawn now—deepslate layers. You want diamonds, you earn them."

Twenty blocks in, Gear's torch supply started running low. "We've got about sixty torches left," he said. "That's maybe four more tunnels."

Red nodded. "Conserve them. One torch every five blocks instead of three. And if someone finds diamonds, we consolidate."

Brick was on the third branch tunnel when everything went wrong.

He swung his pick into the deepslate, the block crumbling into cobble, and suddenly—*hiss.*

The green-and-black body materialized out of the darkness behind the broken block. A creeper. Staring at him with those empty black eyes. Its body began to puff, white cracks forming along its surface.

"CREEPER!" Brick shouted, scrambling backward. But in his panic, he forgot where the tunnel opening was. He'd dug fifteen blocks deep, the narrow 1x2 passage offering no room to maneuver.

*HISS-POP-POP-POP-POP-*

Stone moved before anyone could react. He shoved Brick aside, raising his shield at the last possible second. The explosion rocked the tunnel, sending chunks of deepslate flying. Stone skidded backward, his shield cracked down the middle but intact.

"Everyone okay?" Red shouted through the dust.

Stone coughed. "Shield's toast. But we're alive."

Brick was pale, breathing hard. "I... thanks, man."

"Don't mention it." Stone tossed the ruined shield aside. "Just watch where you're swinging."

Pick inspected the blast crater. "Good news—the creeper opened up more exposed blocks. Bad news—it collapsed part of the main tunnel. We'll need to reinforce."

Gear was already placing support pillars—cobblestone columns every three blocks to prevent cave-ins. "Should have done this from the start," he muttered. "Deepslate's dense but it doesn't hold well when you disturb the structural integrity."

"You sound like you've done this before," Red said.

"I read a book once." Gear shrugged. "Building with Stability. By this guy named Umber. Good stuff."

---

They pushed deeper. The hunger bar was dropping—fast. Down at Y=-58, you burn calories just standing still. The constant mining, the stress, the narrow escapes—it all added up.

Brick pulled out a loaf of bread from his inventory, only to find three bites left. "Anyone got extra food?"

"Half a steak," Stone said, tossing it over.

"Two cookies," Gear offered.

"I've got nothing," Pick said. "I assumed we'd be faster."

Red did a quick mental count. "We've got maybe two more tunnels worth of energy. After that, we go hungry. And hungry means slow mining. Slow mining means more time down here. More time means more mobs."

"More mobs means more creeping," Brick added sheepishly.

"We push forward," Red said. "Three more tunnels. If we don't find anything, we regroup and come back with better supplies."

The fifth tunnel was Pick's. He'd been methodical—swing, step, swing, step, torch, swing. His pickaxe was down to half durability, the iron head wearing thin. But he didn't slow down. He couldn't.

*Clink.*

The sound was different. Higher-pitched. Cleaner.

Pick froze.

"Everyone stop," he said quietly.

He broke the block in front of him. There, embedded in the deepslate like a star in the night sky, was a cluster of light-blue gems. Diamonds. Three of them, nestled together, their facets catching the torchlight and scattering it across the tunnel walls.

"I—" Pick swallowed. "I found them."

The crew crowded around the narrow tunnel, peering over each other's shoulders. Red let out a low whistle. Stone grinned, the first real smile any of them had seen from him all day.

"Three diamonds," Gear said. "We did it."

"Keep digging," Red said, his voice steady but his hands trembling. "There's more."

They widened the area around the diamond cluster, Pick taking point with his pickaxe while the others kept watch. And then—

*Clink.*

Another cluster. Two diamonds this time, deeper in the wall, hidden behind a vein of lapis lazuli.

"That's five," Stone said. "Five diamonds."

"Branch mining works," Pick said, almost to himself. "The math. The spacing. The Y-level. It actually works."

"I never doubted you," Red said. And from the look in his eyes, he meant it.

---

A hiss echoed from somewhere to their left. Then another. A lava pocket had opened up in the next tunnel over, a river of molten rock spilling across the floor. The heat hit them like a wall.

"Back to the main shaft!" Red shouted. "Move!"

They scrambled through the tunnels, Gear grabbing the last of the torches as they ran. The lava pursued them, but it flowed slowly, lazily, across the deepslate floor.

At the base of the central shaft, Red called for a headcount. "Stone? Brick? Gear? Pick?"

All accounted for. All breathing hard.

"We got the diamonds," Brick said, still panting. "Let's get the hell out of here."

The climb back was brutal. Every muscle ached. Their hunger bars were flashing red, hearts thumping warning beats. But nobody complained. Not even Brick.

When they finally broke the surface, the sun was setting over the plains. The base's chimney smoke rose against an orange sky. A chicken wandered past, clucking indifferently.

Red held the five diamonds in his palm, turning them over. They were flawless—deep blue with that telltale shimmer that made every miner's heart skip.

"An enchantment table needs two diamonds in the recipe, plus four obsidian and a book," he said. "These five diamonds give us two for the table, one for the pickaxe, one for a sword, and one for luck."

"One for luck," Stone repeated, nodding.

Back in the base, Gear started cooking the last of their food while Pick sketched out a design for an enchantment room—a 15x15 space with bookshelves arranged in the optimal L-shape pattern for maximum level thirty enchantments.

"We'll need thirty bookshelves total," Pick said, calculating on his fingers. "That's ninety leather and two hundred and seventy paper. But once it's built, we can enchant at level thirty from a single lapis charge."

Red laid the diamonds on the crafting table. The glowstone lamps seemed to burn brighter, reflecting off the gems.

"Tomorrow," Red said, "we build the enchantment table. Tonight..."

He looked at the crew—dirty, exhausted, bruised from creeper encounters and scraping through deepslate tunnels.

"Tonight, we celebrate."

Brick pulled out a jug of suspicious stew that Gear had brewed from brown mushrooms and a dandelion. "To Pick's crazy tunnel math," he said, raising the jug.

"To branch mining," the crew echoed.

And somewhere beneath them, at Y-level negative fifty-eight, the tunnels they'd carved sat in the darkness—the first of many in what would become one of the most successful mining operations in the Overworld.

But that's another story.

**MINING TIPS FROM THIS CHAPTER:**

1. **Y=-58 is the optimal level** for diamond mining in modern Minecraft (1.18+). Diamonds generate most frequently in deepslate layers between Y=-64 and Y=-16, with the highest concentration at Y=-58.

2. **Branch mining at 2x1 tunnels every 3 blocks** maximizes exposed faces per block mined. Each tunnel reveals four blocks per meter: two walls, one ceiling, one floor. The three-block gap means you see every possible diamond vein without digging unnecessary stone.

3. **Place torches on one side of the tunnel only** (e.g., right wall) to always know which direction leads out. If torches are on your right going in, they'll be on your left coming back.

4. **Bring extra shields and food.** Explosions and starvation are the #1 and #2 killers in deep mining. One shield per person is not enough.

5. **Support your tunnels with pillars** every 3-4 blocks in deepslate areas if you're disturbing large volumes of stone. Cave-ins are rare but catastrophic.
