# Chapter 9: Wither War

## Part I: The Restless

The Ender Dragon was dead. The End gateway portals shimmered in silent testimony to what they'd accomplished. For the first time in weeks, the crew of Build Team Zero sat in their base without a single urgent objective hanging over their heads.

It was driving them insane.

"Pass the potatoes," Red said, not looking up from his inventory screen. He was reorganizing his chests for the fourth time that morning.

"You don't even like potatoes," Stone said from the crafting table, where he was systematically turning stacks of iron into anvils they didn't need.

"Doesn't matter. They need to be sorted."

Gear was tinkering with redstone components, building and dismantling the same comparator clock on loop. Brick was pacing. Pick had mined out an entire chunk of stone for no reason other than that he couldn't sit still.

"We beat the Ender Dragon," Pick said, breaking the silence. "That was supposed to be the end."

"It was an end," Stone replied. "Not *the* end."

"Philosophical. Love it." Red slammed a chest shut. "But I'm losing my mind. We've got full diamond gear. We've got shulker boxes. We've got more enchanted books than the Library of Congress. Now what? Build a nice cottage? Pick flowers?"

Brick snorted. "I'd rather fight another dragon."

"Me too," said Gear, finally looking up from his redstone. "But you can't fight the same dragon twice."

Red was about to reply when his hand brushed against something in his ender chest — a book he'd picked up in the Nether fortress during their blaze rod run. He'd almost forgotten about it. The cover was worn, the pages yellowed. He pulled it out and flipped through it.

Then he stopped.

"Hey. Come look at this."

The others gathered around. The page was covered in rough diagrams — a T-shape pattern made of blocks with strange symbols drawn around it. And at the bottom, in jagged charcoal script, three words:

*THE WITHER PROJECT*

"This," Red said slowly, "is a blueprint. An ancient one. From the piglions. Or something older, maybe."

Stone read over his shoulder. "What does it do?"

"Three Wither Skeleton Skulls. Four blocks of Soul Sand. Arranged in a T. You get..." Red's eyes widened. "A boss. The Wither. Supposedly the most dangerous thing in existence."

"More dangerous than the dragon?" Brick asked.

"Different kind of dangerous. The dragon flies. The Wither *destroys*."

Silence fell over the room. Then Pick grinned.

"One more job."

---

## Part II: Back to Hell

The Nether portal flared to life, its purple surface rippling like water. On the other side lay the crimson forest they'd grown so familiar with during their blaze runs. But the fortress — a sprawling network of dark stone bridges and corridors — wasn't in the crimson forest. It was somewhere deeper, somewhere worse.

"Everyone stocked?" Stone asked, adjusting his shield. "Fire resistance potions?"

"Two full stacks," Gear confirmed.

"Golden apples?"

"Sixteen."

"Sword sharpness?"

"Five," Brick said, holding up his diamond blade. "We're ready."

"What nobody's asking," Red said, stepping through the portal, "is how long this is going to take. Because Wither Skeletons don't exactly drop their skulls on command."

He wasn't kidding.

The fortress was massive — bridges spanning across lava lakes, corridors twisting into darkness, spawners rattling in the depths. But the wither skeletons were rare. And the skulls? Those were a one-in-forty drop. Maybe worse.

Hour one was optimistic. The crew cleared three corridors, fought off a dozen blazes, and collected exactly zero skulls.

"Keep going," Stone said. "We knew this wouldn't be easy."

Hour two, Brick almost died.

A wither skeleton caught him from behind while he was fighting a blaze. The blackened arrow struck him square in the back, and the Wither effect — that creeping, starving decay — set in before he could reach for his milk bucket. His health bar shrank. His heart rate spiked.

"Brick! MILK!"

Pick tackled him with a bucket, forcing it into his hands. Brick chugged it as his screen blurred. His health stopped at four hearts.

"I'm fine," he gasped.

"You got hit," Stone said flatly.

"I said I'm fine."

Hour four, Brick almost died again. A misstep on a bridge sent him tumbling into the lava below. He caught the edge of the stone with his fingernails, his legs burning in the molten rock. Gear grabbed his arm and hauled him up while Pick frantically dumped fire resistance on him.

"I'm starting to think this fortress has it out for me," Brick muttered, his armor smoking.

Hour six. No skulls. The grind was wearing thin.

"This is insane," Gear said, leaning against a nether brick wall. "We've killed over a hundred of these things. Not a single skull."

"The drop rate is rough," Red admitted. "We might need to rethink this."

Pick had been quiet for a while. Now he spoke up. "I've got an idea."

---

## Part III: The Farm

Pick's idea was simple: build a dedicated wither skeleton farm.

"Fortresses have spawn blocks," he explained, clearing a wide platform above the fortress's central bridge. "If we clear out all the other spawn spaces — block off the tunnels, light up the adjacent areas — the game is forced to spawn wither skeletons in the space we've prepared."

"It's called spawn-proofing," Stone said, nodding. "Clever."

"Then we add a killing chamber," Pick continued. "Water streams funnel them to a drop chute. We stand at the bottom and swing. Efficient. Safe."

Brick raised an eyebrow. "Safe for who?"

"Us. Not safe for the wither skeletons."

The construction took two hours. They mined, they placed, they lit up nearby tunnels, they redirected lava flows. By the time they were done, they had a compact, efficient farm — a small platform where wither skeletons would spawn, separated from the crew by a waist-high wall.

The first skull dropped within ten minutes.

"There it is!" Red grabbed it — a blackened bone skull with hollow eye sockets. "One down. Two to go."

The second skull came twenty minutes later. The third took another forty-five.

Brick had a system now — stand, swing, watch for the drop, repeat. It was monotonous. It was boring. But compared to another near-death experience in lava? He'd take boring.

"Got it!" Gear held up the third skull, his voice cracking with exhaustion and excitement. "That's all three."

The crew stared at the skulls — three identical trophies resting in their inventory slots. They'd done it. Hours of grinding, brushes with death, the monotony of the farm. But they had what they needed.

"Now comes the hard part," Stone said quietly.

---

## Part IV: The Summoning

They built the chamber deep underground — a hundred blocks beneath their base, carved into the stone with painstaking precision. The walls were obsidian. The floor was obsidian. The ceiling was obsidian. If there was a material that could survive the Wither's explosion, this was it.

"Four blocks of soul sand," Red read from the ancient blueprint, his voice echoing in the hollow space. "Bottom row of three. Top row of one. Center block."

Stone placed the soul sand. The T-shape rose from the obsidian floor like an altar.

"Then the skulls," Red continued. "On top of the three bottom blocks."

"Wait," Brick said. "Does it matter who places them?"

"There's no lore on that. But I think..." Red's voice dropped. "I think we all should."

Stone placed the first skull. The air grew heavy.

Gear placed the second. The torchlight flickered.

Pick placed the third. A low rumble vibrated through the stone.

Red stepped forward. The last skull sat in his hand — the center block of the T. He looked at his crew.

"Whatever happens next," he said, "we do this together."

He placed the skull.

For a moment, nothing happened. Then the world *screamed*.

The sky — or what passed for sky a hundred blocks underground — went black. The torches died. The obsidian walls trembled. And in the center of the room, the T-shape began to collapse inward, the blocks and skulls swirling into a vortex of darkness and purple particles.

Then the explosion.

The Wither erupted into being with a blast that shattered the first layer of obsidian. It was massive — three heads, each with hollow white eyes, a skeletal body wreathed in dark smoke. Its roar shook the very chunk data of the world.

"IT'S HERE!" Brick screamed, barely audible over the cacophony.

The Wither's heads swiveled. Its eye sockets found them. And it *attacked*.

---

## Part V: Chaos

Blue skulls flew in every direction — homing projectiles that detonated on impact like delayed TNT. The first one hit the wall and blew a hole through the obsidian. The second one hit the floor and sent Stone flying.

"I can't see!" Gear shouted, diving behind a pillar.

"Keep moving!" Red yelled back. "Don't let it line up a shot!"

Brick charged. It was stupid. It was brave. It was absolutely insane.

He got two hits in before a blue skull caught him in the chest and launched him across the chamber. His health bar dropped by half. The Wither effect crept in — the blackened veins spreading across his vision.

"Brick, fall back!" Pick fired arrow after arrow at the Wither, but the projectiles seemed to do nothing. "The arrows — they're barely scratching it!"

"Keep firing!" Stone was on his feet again, shield raised, sword ready. "Every hit counts!"

The battle was chaos. The lava they'd placed around the perimeter as a defensive measure was now a hazard — the Wither's explosions sent blocks flying, creating new pathways, destroying their carefully prepared terrain. The crew was scattered. The Wither was relentless.

And then it happened.

The Wither's health dropped to half.

Its body flashed. The dark smoke around it turned into a shimmering white barrier. Its eyes blazed brighter. And a new sound — a high-pitched, piercing scream — filled the chamber.

"It's changing!" Gear shouted.

"IT'S IMMUNE!" Red's voice cracked. "The book said it — at half health — it becomes immune to arrows!"

Pick's bow went slack. "You're joking."

"I'M NOT JOKING!"

The Wither's attacks intensified. The blue skulls came faster. The explosions were bigger. The crew was pinned — scattered, out of position, and now their primary ranged weapon was useless.

Brick looked at his sword. He looked at the Wither. He looked at his friends.

"I guess it's melee time," he said, and charged again.

---

## Part VI: The Breaking Point

It went on for what felt like hours. The obsidian chamber was unrecognizable — craters everywhere, lava spilling through broken walls, the ceiling partially collapsed. The Wither flew through the destruction like a god of annihilation.

Brick hit it, got blown back, got up, hit it again.

Stone coordinated — shouting positions, calling out incoming skulls, keeping the crew from dying in the crossfire.

Gear used the environment — setting up barriers, redirecting the Wither's path, buying precious seconds.

Pick — without his arrows — grabbed a sword and fought beside Brick, their blades flashing in time.

Red ran the skulls. When a Wither skeleton skull dropped from the chaos — a summoned minion of the boss — he grabbed it, pocketed it, and threw himself back into the fight.

"Focus on the body!" Stone shouted. "The heads are armored — hit the torso!"

One by one, the crew closed in. Brick was at half a heart, his armor broken, his sword shattered. He grabbed a replacement from his inventory and kept swinging.

The Wither roared. Its barrier flickered. Its health bar — nearly invisible, nearly gone — hung at the edge of death.

"One more push!" Red screamed. "EVERYONE! NOW!"

The five of them struck as one. Brick's sword. Pick's axe. Stone's critical hit. Gear's sweeping edge. Red's fire aspect.

The Wither convulsed. Its three heads let out a final, deafening scream. And then — silence.

The explosion was soft — a gentle burst of white light that swept through the chamber like a wave. The smoke cleared. The darkness lifted.

And there, floating in the center of the ruined room, was a single black object. Glowing. Pulsing. Beautiful.

The Nether Star.

Nobody moved. Nobody spoke.

Finally, Gear stepped forward. His hand trembled as he picked it up. The star hummed with energy — raw, pure, unfathomable.

"This," Gear said, his voice barely a whisper, "is going to make something INCREDIBLE."

---

## Part VII: The Beacon

Three days later, they stood on the surface, watching Gear work.

The obsidian base was a pyramid. Not a building — an actual pyramid, three layers of obsidian rising from the earth. On top of it, Gear placed the first block. Then the second. Then the third.

The Beacon block — a device of pure glass, obsidian, and the Nether Star — sat at the apex.

"Ready?" Gear asked.

"Ready," the crew said together.

Gear flipped the switch.

A beam of light erupted from the Beacon, punching through the sky, through the clouds, through the build limit itself. It was visible for hundreds of blocks — a column of brilliance connecting earth and heaven.

The crew watched in silence. They were battered, bruised, exhausted, and victorious.

"One more job," Red said, laughing. "We always say that."

"Maybe this time," Stone said, "we actually relax."

Brick snorted. "Relaxing is boring."

"Then you can farm more wither skeletons," Pick said.

"Pass."

Gear was still staring at the Beacon. "You know what this means, right? We've got the star. We can make any effect we want. Speed, strength, regeneration, resistance — all of it. We can build more Beacons. We can build an entire network."

"A network of Beacons?" Stone asked.

"Why not? We've been fighting monsters. Maybe it's time to build something bigger."

The beam of light shone over Build Team Zero's base, a monument to their greatest battle yet. But even as they stood there, proud and exhausted, a thought lingered in the back of each of their minds:

*Something bigger is coming.*

They just didn't know what.

But the Beacon would be ready.
