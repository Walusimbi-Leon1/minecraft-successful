# Chapter 4: Emerald Exchange

**Players:** Stone · Pick · Gear  
**Mission:** Find a village, set up a trading hall, and trade for 32 emeralds  
**Reward:** 3 Emeralds each + Hero of the Village

---

The sun was a line of gold bleeding across the eastern horizon as Stone, Pick, and their new companion crested the ridge.

Below them, a cluster of sandstone and oak buildings sat nestled in the crook of a river. Wheat fields stretched in neat rows. A bell tower rose from the center. Sheep grazed lazily by the banks.

"A village," Pick breathed, shielding his eyes. "An actual, honest-to-Herobrine village."

Stone nodded, already counting. "At least a dozen houses. Maybe two dozen villagers. That's a lot of trades."

"Trades we can't make without a proper setup," said the third figure.

Gear was shorter than both of them, with goggles pushed up on his forehead and a redstone torch dangling from his belt. His chestplate was etched with circuits—glowing lines of copper and lapis that pulsed with a faint hum. Everything about him moved with the efficiency of a well-oiled piston.

"I've been designing this for weeks," he said, pulling a scroll from his pack. The paper unrolled to reveal detailed schematics—lecterns, grindstones, brewing stands, and a dozen other workstation layouts. "Give me an hour and these villagers will be trading their grandmas for a single stick."

"You're terrifying," Pick said.

"I prefer *efficient*."

---

## The Plains Village of Oakrest

They descended into the village as the roosters began to crow. A farmer villager near the river looked up, tilted its head, and grunted. A butcher leaned out of a stone house, meat cleaver in hand, before deciding the newcomers weren't worth the effort.

Stone walked the perimeter, sword out, scanning the flat plains. The village was vulnerable—no walls, no fortifications, just open fields in every direction. A few wolves roamed near the outskirts, but they'd be useless against anything serious.

"We're exposed here," he said when he returned.

"We won't be long," Gear replied, already dropping his pack in the center of the village. "Two hours. Maybe three. Then we're gone."

Pick had his eye on the villagers. "The farmer has potatoes. The librarian has four bookshelves. I saw a weaponsmith—"

"Focus on emeralds," Stone said. "That's the mission."

"Always the mission with you."

Stone's jaw tightened. "That's how we survive."

Gear ignored them both. He was already placing blocks—a lectern here, a grindstone there, a smithing table near the blacksmith's house. He worked with the careful precision of someone who had done this a hundred times in his head.

"Pick, I need you to break those hay bales and trade with the farmer first. Emeralds for wheat. We'll convert through the librarian for enchanted books, then sell those to the weaponsmith. Three-layer conversion. Clean."

"Clean," Pick repeated, grinning. "I like it."

"You'd like anything that doesn't involve swinging a pickaxe for once," Stone muttered.

"Not wrong."

Gear snapped his fingers. "Move. The market doesn't build itself."

---

## The Trading Begins

The morning turned into a rhythm.

Pick worked the farmer—wheat for emeralds, emeralds for more wheat, cycling through the trades until the farmer's stock depleted and restocked. Gear stood by with a notepad, tracking every transaction, optimizing the flow like a server managing traffic.

"We're at eight emeralds," Gear announced after the first twenty minutes. "Pick, switch to the librarian. Stone—I need more paper. The cartographer has a trade locked behind exploration maps. If we can unlock it—"

"On it."

Stone scoured the village, collecting sugarcane from the riverbank, crafting paper, feeding the cartographer's demand. The trades stacked. Eight emeralds became twelve. Twelve became eighteen.

The villagers grunted and squeaked their approval. One of the farmer's children threw a poppy at Pick's feet.

"See?" Pick said, pocketing two more emeralds. "They like me."

"They like your emeralds," Stone said.

"Same thing."

Gear had established three workstations by now, each one carefully positioned to maximize workflow. The lectern faced the center square. The grindstone was under a roof overhang for shade. The smithing table sat next to a lava pit he'd dug himself, the glow casting long shadows across the cobblestone.

"Twenty-four," Gear called out. "Eight to go."

Pick wiped sweat from his brow. "I could do this all day."

"Don't," Stone said, eyeing the horizon. "Something's off."

The winds had shifted.

The sheep had stopped grazing.

---

## The Pillagers

The first crossbow bolt buried itself in the dirt three feet from Gear's head.

"INCOMING!"

The war horn split the air—a deep, guttural blast that sent chickens scattering and villagers screaming. From the northern treeline, a column of gray figures emerged. Ragged banners. Pale, stitched skin. Crossbows glinting in the midday sun.

Pillagers.

A full patrol. No—not a patrol. A *raid*.

"Nine of them!" Pick shouted, counting. "Ten—no, twelve!"

"We need to evacuate the villagers—" Stone started.

"No time." Gear had already dropped his notepad and was pulling a bow from his pack. "If they trigger the raid horn, more will come. We end this here. Fast."

Stone grabbed his shield from his back, the wood creaking as he locked it onto his arm. "Pick—the church roof. High ground."

"You read my mind."

They ran.

---

## The Defense of Oakrest

Pick took the bell tower in three leaps, boots finding footing on windowsills and crossbeams until he was perched on the wooden balcony, bow drawn. Below, the pillagers spread into a loose formation—disciplined, practiced. These weren't random wanderers. They knew what they were doing.

"Pick! Left flank!"

Pick pivoted. A pillager with an iron-tipped arrow was taking aim at a farmer frozen in the middle of the road. Pick let his arrow fly—a clean, whistling shot that caught the raider in the shoulder. The pillager stumbled, the shot going wide, shattering a window.

"Get inside!" Pick roared at the farmer.

The villager scrambled.

Stone was on the ground, shield raised, advancing. Crossbow bolts thudded against his shield like heavy rain. One. Two. Three. His arm numbed with each impact, but he didn't stop. He couldn't.

"Gear—the avenues! Block the gaps!"

Gear was already moving. He pulled blocks of cobblestone from his inventory and slammed them into the gaps between houses, sealing off the alleyways. The pillagers were funneled now—forced into the main square where Stone stood waiting.

"Ten seconds," Gear yelled. "Give me ten seconds."

Stone drew his sword.

The first pillager rounded the corner. Stone stepped inside its range before it could fire, his blade finding the gap under its arm. The creature crumpled. Two more followed. Stone banged his sword against his shield—a challenge.

"COME ON!"

They came.

Pick's arrows rained from above, taking down the raiders who tried to circle around. A vindicator charged with an axe raised high—Pick dropped him with an arrow through the knee. The vindicator howled, collapsed, and Gear finished him with a well-placed stone sword.

"Twenty-two!" Gear shouted. Wait—he'd been counting emeralds. Now he counted bodies.

A ravager—massive, horned, snorting smoke—stamped into the square. Its rider raised a banner and the remaining pillagers rallied.

"Stone! Take the rider!"

Stone didn't hesitate. He charged, shield low, sword angled. The ravager stomped—he rolled, came up under its neck, and slashed across the rider's leg. The banner bearer tumbled, and Pick's arrow found its mark before the raider hit the ground.

The ravager turned, confused without its handler.

Stone sheathed his sword, drew his bow, and put three arrows into its skull.

The beast fell.

Silence.

---

## Aftermath

The raid horn lay shattered in the dirt.

Pick slid down the church roof, landing with a puff of dust. Gear leaned against a workbench, chest heaving. Stone stood in the center of the square, surrounded by pillager bodies, shield cracked, sword dripping.

One by one, the villagers emerged.

The farmer. The butcher. The weaponsmith. The librarian. A child clutching a stuffed sheep.

They stared at the bodies. Then at Stone.

And then the farmer bowed.

It spread like a wave. The butcher bowed. The librarian. The cartographer. Every villager in Oakrest bent at the waist, offering a gesture of respect that Stone had never seen from a villager before.

"What is this?" he asked quietly.

Particles of light began to swirl around them—golden, warm, like fireflies at dusk. The air hummed with an energy that made Stone's skin prickle.

"Hero of the Village," Gear whispered. "I read about it. Never thought I'd see it."

A villager approached—the old librarian, his spectacles askew, his robes torn from the chaos. In his trembling hands, he held out an emerald block. Not a single gem. A full block.

"Oh," Pick breathed. "Oh, that's beautiful."

Then came the rest. Enchanted books. An iron sword. Bread—dozens of loaves. The villagers pressed gifts into their hands, chattering in their guttural language, pointing at the sky where the particles swirled, offering everything they had.

Gear counted the emeralds they'd already collected. "Twenty-eight," he said.

Stone knelt, accepting an emerald from a small child. He looked at Gear.

"Twenty-nine."

Pick laughed, pulling a handful of gems from the donations. "Thirty-two. Done."

"We're done," Stone said. He looked at the villagers, the bread in his hands, the smiling child. "Mission complete."

---

## The Departure

They left Oakrest as the sun began to set, the village behind them bathed in orange light. The villagers had already started rebuilding. Three new iron golems patrolled the perimeter. The farmer's son waved from the bell tower.

"That wasn't so bad," Pick said.

"Speak for yourself." Gear was nursing a bruise on his ribs. "My back hurts, my notepad is ruined, and I'm pretty sure I lost a redstone torch in the river."

"You can build another."

"I can. I will."

Stone walked ahead, silent, the emerald block heavy in his pack. Pick caught up with him.

"You okay?"

Stone didn't answer for a long moment. Then: "They didn't have walls. That village. They were sitting ducks."

"So?"

"So, next time—" He stopped. Shook his head. "Never mind."

Gear and Pick exchanged a glance.

"The grindstone's in my blood," Stone said finally. "But that doesn't mean I want to watch people lose everything."

Pick clapped him on the shoulder. "That's almost human of you, Stone."

"Shut up."

"Hero of the Village."

"I said *shut up*."

Gear laughed—a rare, rusty sound. The three of them walked into the setting sun, packs full of emeralds, the memory of a grateful village warm at their backs.

Behind them, the bell of Oakrest tolled once.

Not a warning.

A thank you.
